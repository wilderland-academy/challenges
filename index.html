    Explore the life skills

    Show only

    K–1 is for guides. Every K–1 challenge is led by a guide with the whole crew. Some fit in a day, and some run over several days or weeks.

    • Each child aims to pass at least 3 challenges in each of the 8 life skills this year, so plan enough for every child to get there, retries included.
    • Run at least 2 life skill challenges a week.
    • Finish each one with a quick lightning test. Not every child has to pass every challenge.

    These are here to inspire you. Make up your own challenges whenever you have a better idea for your crew. Just make sure each one builds one of the 8 life skills.

    How it works

    1. Pick

      Choose a challenge and tell your Field Guide your plan.

    2. Practice

      Use the “Where to learn” links on each challenge, and ask for help.

    3. Show it

      Show your guide in person, any day you’re ready, or in the lightning test at the end of a Lab. Challenges marked Can do at home can also be passed off with a parent’s video or log. A few need big setup, like a pool swim, so those get a scheduled test day.

    4. Pass, or not yet

      No partial credit and no limit on retries. A not yet tells you exactly what to practice.

    5. Rank up

      Every pass is a credit. Credits move you up your trail.

    Bring your hobby

    Into music, drawing, coding, games, a sport or birds? Lots of challenges can be done through the thing you already love, at a harder level than you’d push yourself alone. Look for the Bring your hobby tag, or search for your hobby: challenges about it come first, and challenges you can do through it are listed after them.

    Four words to know

    Challenge
    One “I can” statement with a clear standard.
    Credit
    One passed challenge from your grade band or higher.
    Epic
    A bigger build over several weeks, with 3 checkpoints. Worth 2 credits.
    Gate
    A safety check you pass before an activity, like your fire card before you light a fire.

    Everyone starts at the bottom of their own trail, whatever their age. After grades 1 and 4 a crossing ceremony moves you to the next trail, and your credits come with you. You receive each rank at Honor Court, with your feat named out loud. The passport means one challenge each with a real audience, a night outdoors, teaching someone, leading a group, a hard physical feat, and building with tech. Rank names are working names; the Founders Crew picks the final ones.

    About “not yet”

    You will hear it a lot. So does everyone who gets good at anything. A not yet comes with what was missing, how to practice it, and a retry date. Before a hard test your heart will race. That’s your body sending oxygen to your brain and muscles. Use it.

    Passing off at homeHigh standards, high support

    Parents can pass off some challenges from home: a video for skills your child can show on demand, a same-day log for habits, and a witness note for moments only you see. A pass from home is worth exactly the same as a pass at school, so it’s held to exactly the same sentence.

    Read the parent guide
    • Video for skills shown on demand, like a timed run, a knot or a talk
    • Parent log for habits over days or weeks, written the same day
    • Witness note for real moments you see, like ordering food on their own
    • Safety Gates always need a guide or certified instructor, never a parent
